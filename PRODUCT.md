# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The broad art audience discovering Sabrina Suppa online — often arriving cold from social media, a link, or a search, frequently on a phone. They are not art-world insiders being sold to; they are strangers deciding, in a few seconds, whether this work is worth their attention. Their context is a scroll among a thousand other images. The job to be done is atmospheric before it is informational: they need to *feel* the world of the work — its strangeness, its intelligence — fast enough that they stay, then move fluidly from that first impression into the pieces themselves (Adaptive Flesh, Physical) and, eventually, into who made them.

Secondary readers — curators, collaborators, press — pass through the same front door. Nothing here is tailored to them at the broad audience's expense, but the seriousness that satisfies them is a byproduct of getting the atmosphere right, not a separate track.

## Product Purpose

A single-artist portfolio for **Sabrina Suppa — "Body Architect,"** whose practice explores *adaptive morphologies*: engineered structures that reshape and extend the human silhouette, sitting at the seam of sculpture, wearable architecture, and speculative biology. The site exists to give that practice a home online that is itself an artifact of the practice — not a neutral container for images, but an extension of the work's own logic (the body made strange, protection and mutation, controlled adaptation).

Success looks like a visitor arriving cold and staying: reaching the work, feeling the unease deliberately, and leaving with a specific, unforgettable impression of *this* artist — not a general sense of "nice portfolio." The site should read as authored, singular, and impossible to mistake for a template.

## Brand Personality

Three words: **visceral, clinical, controlled.** The work is about the body under engineering — flesh treated as a material to be calibrated, protected, transformed — and the interface carries that same charged tension: an unsettling subject handled with cold precision. Voice is spare and exact; copy reads like a research statement or a museum caption, never like marketing. The emotional target is a *refined discomfort* — the body made strange, a fascination that is slightly uncomfortable — achieved through restraint and atmosphere, never through shock effects.

## Anti-references

- **Generic Squarespace / template artist portfolio.** A white grid of thumbnails, a neutral sans-serif, no point of view. The single biggest failure mode: this must never read as a picked-a-theme portfolio. Identity is the whole point.
- **Startup / SaaS polish.** Bright, friendly, rounded corners, gradient accents, "hero-metric" energy, product-landing-page cadence. Anything that reads as software marketing is wrong for a sculptural practice.
- **Gothic / horror kitsch.** The unease is refined and clinical, not theatrical. No skulls, gore, blood-drip, or Halloween darkness. Dark because the work lives in cinematic low light, not because "dark = edgy."
- **Busy / maximalist.** No clutter, no loud stacking, no animation for its own sake. Nothing on screen should compete with the work. Motion is cinematic and orchestrated, never decorative noise.

## Design Principles

- **The site is the work's extension, not its frame.** Every choice should feel authored by the same intelligence that made the sculptures — adaptation, calibration, controlled mutation as interface behavior, not decoration bolted on.
- **Unease by restraint, not by shock.** The visceral charge comes from precision, low light, and the body-material palette — never from gore or theatrics. Discomfort should feel intentional and refined.
- **Let the work hold the room.** Imagery leads; the interface recedes. Typography, spacing, and motion exist to frame and pace the pieces, not to draw attention to themselves.
- **Atmosphere before information.** A cold visitor should feel the world in seconds, then be carried into the work. Legibility and navigation serve that arc; they never announce themselves first.
- **Singular, not repeatable.** No move should be reusable as generic "portfolio grammar." If a competitor's site could use the same pattern unchanged, reconsider it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aesthetics lead; accessibility is best-effort rather than a hard gate. In practice that means: keep the cinematic, motion-rich art direction as the priority, but do not let it break the experience for people who can't take it. Retain a `prefers-reduced-motion` path so the site remains usable (crossfade/instant rather than the full choreography), keep interactive controls (gallery navigation, nav, menu) keyboard-reachable, and preserve the descriptive, voice-forward alt text already in the copy. Where the low-light palette pushes text contrast below AA, favor the art direction — but bump body copy toward the bone-porcelain end of the ramp when a fix is free. Treat contrast shortfalls as known, deliberate trade-offs, not accidents.
